Attestat Showcase: payout desk

Pay your freelancers. Never hold their passports.

A company pays its freelance contractors in stablecoins from a shared treasury wallet. The treasury can pay adults who have shown state identity evidence, and nobody else. The company never holds an identity document: the contractor's EUDI presentation becomes an eligibility decision on chain, bound to the payout address, and the payout contract refuses any address without a live decision.

"Both officers approved. Privy allowed it. The chain said no."

Who buys, who uses

The finance desk buys. Two officers and the contractor use it.

The buyer

The finance function of a company with freelance contractors in the EU, or a contractor-payments vendor that sells to such companies. It already runs a payout process. It wants a rule the treasury enforces, and it does not want a folder of identity copies.

The users

Officer A proposes a payout run. Officer B approves it. The contractor presents identity evidence once from the state wallet and is paid on every run after that, as long as the decision is live.

The money

A euro or dollar stablecoin moves from the company's treasury wallet to the contractor's own address. In the demo it is mUSD, a test token with six decimals, on Sepolia.

Why the desk pays for it

Today it either runs a document funnel and keeps an ID copy for every freelancer, or it pays a bare address with no assurance about who is behind it. Neither is a rule a treasury can enforce. This desk has one: pay addresses with a live decision, stop when the decision is revoked or expires. The name for the invoice record still reaches the company from the presentation, never from the chain.

Otherwise not possible

Three facts that block this today.

  1. A contract with a minor stays pending until the legal representative approves it, and member states must prohibit work by children, so a company that pays a self-hosted address cannot know whether the person behind it may contract or work at all; over 18 is the predicate the law itself defines, so a predicate answer settles it.

    German Civil Code, sections 106 to 108; Council Directive 94/33/EC

  2. A company paying contractors is not an obliged entity under the EU anti-money-laundering regulation, so it owes no customer due diligence, has no legal basis to collect and keep identity documents, and is told by data-minimisation to keep less; it still wants assurance about age and capacity.

    Regulation (EU) 2024/1624, Article 3; GDPR Article 5(1)(c)

  3. A wallet policy engine can evaluate the transaction, the calldata, typed data and the request body, and none of those fields says who controls the recipient address, so a treasury rule about identity cannot be written in the wallet policy without an on-chain predicate to read.

    Privy policy reference, field sources ethereum_transaction, ethereum_calldata, ethereum_typed_data, action_request_body

2027

Member states must offer the wallet by the end of 2026. Private relying parties in financial services must accept it from the end of 2027 (eIDAS 2, Article 5f). A plain employer is not obliged to accept the wallet. A payment institution that runs payroll is.

How it works

One decision on chain. The payout is its third door.

The fund token and the Uniswap permissioned pool already read the same decision. The payout contract makes the same call. Privy enforces where the money may go. The chain enforces who may receive it.

  1. Contractor

    Present

    The official test wallet on the phone answers the desk's registered request: given name, family name, over 18. The names go to the desk's verifier for the invoice record, not to the chain.

  2. Contractor's browser

    Prove

    The contractor's own browser tab decrypts the relayed response and makes a zero-knowledge proof of identity evidence and over 18 (Noir, UltraHonk). Our server is a blind relay that holds the encrypted response and nothing readable.

  3. Chain, then the desk

    Decide

    The proof is verified on Sepolia and a decision is stored against the payout address: policy, bits, expiry. The finance desk approves in a separate on-chain step, by hand. No name, no document.

  4. Privy

    Pay

    The treasury is a Privy server wallet owned by a two-officer key quorum. Its policy allows eth_sendTransaction to the GatedPayout contract on this chain and denies everything else. Officer A proposes an intent, officer B authorizes it, Privy executes the payout call.

  5. Two refusals

    Refuse

    A direct transfer to a contractor is denied by the Privy policy before it reaches the chain. After the desk revokes a contractor, both officers can still approve a new run and Privy still allows it; the chain reverts with NotEligible for that address, and nothing is paid. Revoke is manual; the gate is on chain, not in our database.

What the chain sees

Six fields. None of them a name.

One decision per address and policy. The payout contract asks the registry one question for each recipient and refuses the whole run if any answer is no.

  • Eligible means identity evidence and over 18, approved, not revoked, not expired. It does not mean one person per address.
  • The desk sees an address, an amount and a yes. The invoice name comes from the presentation it received, not from this record.
  • Privy sees addresses, amounts and calldata. Never identity evidence.
  • Anyone sees the six fields below. Predicate bits and an expiry bound to an address are still personal data; we say so.
// one record per (address, policyId)
struct Decision {
  bytes32 policyId;   // which policy decided
  uint256 bits;       // bit 0 evidence, bit 1 over 18
  uint8   tier;       // issuer-defined, informational
  uint64  expiry;     // unix time, then it stops counting
  bytes32 statusRef;  // opaque status reference, never a name
  bool    revoked;
}

// the gate, same call the fund token and the pool make
for (recipient of recipients)
  if (!registry.isEligible(recipient, policyId, 0x3))
    revert NotEligible(recipient);
  token.transferFrom(treasury, recipient, amount);

Struct from the registry interface in the nachweis-app repository. The payout loop is the GatedPayout contract in prose form.

The demo

Run it on a local chain, or on Sepolia with Privy.

The demo is a route in the Attestat product app. In local mode it runs on anvil without a Privy app and captions the second approval as simulated. In Privy mode on Sepolia the treasury is a real Privy server wallet with a key quorum and a policy, and the builder clicks through it by hand.
