Showcase Standing order

A standing order that stops the moment the decision does.

A fund issuer runs a recurring subscription for an investor from her own wallet and never holds her identity document. Her EUDI evidence produced one eligibility decision on chain, with an expiry and a revoke flag. Privy provides her wallet, a signer on it for the issuer's automation, and a policy on that signer that is a copy of the decision: only this fund's subscribe call, only until the expiry, denied the moment the issuer revokes.

Who buys and who uses

The issuer pays. The investor holds the wallet.

The issuer buys

A fund issuer, transfer agent or launchpad that already gates its token. A standing order turns a one-time subscription into recurring inflow. Today it cannot offer one on a tokenized instrument without running its own document funnel for every wallet and keeping a person in the loop for every run.

Gets: an automation that subscribes on schedule and is stopped by three parties without a document behind any of them: the issuer's revoke, the decision's expiry, the investor's remove.

The investor uses

She signs in by email and gets an embedded wallet by Privy. She presents her sample identity from the official test wallet once. Then she grants the issuer's automation a signer with a policy she can read in plain words, and she can remove that signer at any time.

Gets: a savings plan on a gated fund she can stop with one button, from a wallet she did not have to set up.

Otherwise not possible

Privy can bound a signer. It cannot know who is eligible.

  1. A Privy policy reads transaction fields, decoded calldata, typed data, message content and the system clock, and nothing on chain. "Sign only while this person is eligible" cannot be written in Privy alone; it needs an external, machine-readable fact with an expiry and a revocation signal.

    Source: Privy docs, policies overview, the field_source list (fetched 9 September 2026).

  2. The AttestationRegistry stores exactly that per wallet and policy: an expiry and a revoked flag, an Approved and a Revoked event, and an expiry check on every read. The fund's subscribe call checks the wallet address, not who authorised the signature, so a signer added to the investor's wallet inherits her eligibility and loses it in the same block she does.

    Source: contracts/src/interfaces/IEligibility.sol and contracts/src/AttestationRegistry.sol in the nachweis-app repository.

  3. No live gated product accepts an external attestation without a contract, so today every automated run needs the operator's own allowlist with an identity file behind it. With the decision on chain and the policy copied from it, the run reads a decision, not a person. The issuer keeps its compliance file; a predicate never replaces it.

    Source: the live-gated-products memo of 6 September 2026 (count zero) and the product-pitch-legal memo, both in the Attestat wiki.

How it works

Six beats, and the last one is a refusal.

  1. Investor

    Present

    The official German EUDI test wallet on her phone answers the issuer's registered request with the sample identity: given name, family name, over 18. Names go to the issuer's verifier, not to the chain.

  2. Her browser

    Prove

    The browser tab decrypts the wallet's response and makes a zero-knowledge proof (Noir) that the predicates hold. Proof made in this browser tab. The chain will verify the proof and nothing else.

  3. Chain

    Decide

    The issuer approves in a separate step. One Decision is written against her wallet address: policy id, predicate bits, tier, expiry, status reference, revoked flag. No name, no document.

  4. Issuer's automation, Privy

    Mirror

    The automation sees Approved, reads the decision, and creates a Privy policy for this investor from it. She grants the issuer's signer on her embedded wallet, under that policy. The signer never sees the wallet's private key (Privy's statement).

  5. Issuer's automation, Privy

    Run

    Each month the automation sends subscribe() from her wallet through Privy's wallet API with the issuer's authorization key. Privy checks the policy, signs, broadcasts. The fund's contract checks her decision and mints her units.

  6. Issuer, then Privy and chain

    Revoke

    The issuer revokes on chain. The automation appends a deny-all rule to the policy. The next run is refused by Privy before anything is broadcast, and the chain would refuse it too. She can also remove the signer herself at any time; expiry closes it without anyone acting.

What the chain sees

Six fields. None of them is her.

The registry keeps one Decision per wallet address and policy. The fund's subscribe call and the fund token's transfer check both read it through isEligible: approved, not revoked, not expired, bits present. The automation's signer changes nothing here; the chain sees a transaction from her address and asks the same question it would ask her.

Predicate bits and an expiry bound to an address are still personal data. What is not there: a name, a birth date, a document, or anything we could use to find her.

// one per (subject, policyId); read by the fund and the pool
struct Decision {
  bytes32 policyId;   // the issuer's policy hash
  uint256 bits;       // predicate bits the policy verified
  uint8   tier;       // issuer-defined, informational
  uint64  expiry;     // unix time; read-side check
  bytes32 statusRef;  // opaque status pointer, never a name
  bool    revoked;    // set by the issuer's revoke
}

contracts/src/interfaces/IEligibility.sol. The two fields the Privy policy copies are expiry and revoked.

What the two screens say, verbatim

Investor card

Wallet by Privy: an embedded wallet created at email sign-in. Your identity evidence never goes to Privy. Privy sees this address, the transactions it signs, and one policy: the issuer's automation may send subscribe() for you to this fund until your decision expires on 7 March 2027. When the issuer revokes your decision, its automation closes the policy; you can remove the signer here at any time. Sample identity from the official test wallet; testnet funds.

Issuer panel

Automation by Privy: the issuer's key signs on the investor's wallet only under a policy copied from her on-chain decision. Revoke on chain, then the automation adds a deny rule to that policy. Privy enforces the policy; Privy does not read the chain.

Honest about the demo

  • Official test walletThe German EUDI sandbox with a sample identity. No state-issued ID in this build.
  • Where the proof is madeIn the investor's browser tab (Noir), on her own computer. The official wallet does not produce this proof today.
  • SimulatedSanctions and similar checks are stubs. The scheduler tick is simulated by the Run the month button. In local mode the Privy policy is a local evaluator, captioned as such.
  • Manual revocationThe issuer revokes by hand. The demo fund's subscribe mints 100 NDF without payment.
  • On chainNo identity documents on chain, and nothing we could use to find her.
