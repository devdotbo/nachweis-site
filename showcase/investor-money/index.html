Who buys it, who uses it.

The buyer

The issuer of a gated fund token

A fund issuer, transfer agent or launchpad. Today its primary market runs on its own document funnel and a wallet whitelist for mint and redeem, kept fresh by hand. It buys the desk because the whitelist becomes a decision the chain enforces, and because it stops asking the investor for an address it cannot trust.

The user

An investor with an email address and a state ID wallet

No browser extension, no exchange account, no seed phrase. She has the official test wallet on her phone, with a sample identity, and an email address. She signs in, gets an address, and from then on her wallet holds the fund units and the stablecoin and signs every transaction.

mUSDa test stablecoin, six decimals, anyone can mint it NDFthe demo fund's units, one mUSD per unit The desksubscribe, distribute, claim, redeem, each behind the same check

Otherwise not possible.

In every live tokenized product we surveyed, the whitelist for mint and redeem is built from documents the issuer collected and keeps, so an investor without a crypto wallet is on no list at all.

Source: survey of live gated products, 2026-09-06

Regulation (EU) 2023/1113, Art. 14(5) and 16(2), asks a crypto-asset service provider to assess whether a self-hosted address is owned or controlled by the customer, and EBA/GL/2024/11 paragraph 83(d) accepts a message signed with that address's key.

Source: Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 and EBA/GL/2024/11

Here the address is created at email sign-in, its key signs the session binding, and the EUDI presentation carries a hash of the address and the challenge, so address ownership and the identity predicate are proven in one act while the issuer holds no document.

Source: the demo's code, PresentCard.tsx and handoff.ts in nachweis-app

The limit, stated: a predicate does not replace customer due diligence for an obliged entity (AMLR, Art. 22 and Art. 77). The issuer keeps its compliance file off chain. The desk carries eligibility, not onboarding.

How it works, in six beats.

Two tabs and a phone. The investor's tab on the left, the issuer's on the right, the Privy confirmation in front at every signature.

  1. Privy Sign in with email

    One-time code. Privy creates an embedded wallet and the card shows the address. This is the address everything else binds to.

  2. Phone Present

    The official test wallet answers the issuer's registered request: given name, family name, over 18. Sample identity. The name goes to the issuer, not to the chain.

  3. Browser tab Prove

    The investor's own browser tab makes the zero-knowledge proof over the presentation and the address hash. The chain verifies it and does not trust our server.

  4. Chain Decide

    The issuer matches its record and clicks Approve. A decision is written against the address: policy, bits, tier, expiry. No name, no document.

  5. Privy Move money

    Subscribe, claim, redeem, send. Each is a transaction the embedded wallet signs. The desk checks the decision at that block before any stablecoin or unit moves.

  6. Issuer Revoke

    The issuer withdraws approval by hand. Claim and redeem revert with NotEligible, shown from simulation before any signature. Approve again and they open.

What the chain sees.

One record per wallet and policy. The desk, the fund token's transfer hook and the pool's allowlist checker all ask the same question of it.

  • name
  • email address
  • date of birth
  • document image
  • the presentation

No identity documents on chain, and nothing we could use to find her.

// one per (subject, policyId)
struct Decision {
    bytes32 policyId;   0x9c02…e7b8
    uint256 bits;       over18: 1  evidence: 1
    uint8   tier;       A
    uint64  expiry;     2026-10-07
    bytes32 statusRef;  opaque, never a name
    bool    revoked;    false
}

// the desk, before any money moves
require(isEligible(msg.sender, policyId, bits),
        NotEligible(msg.sender));

The struct from the registry's read interface. The check at the top of subscribe, claim and redeem.

Honest about the demo

  • Official test walletThe German EUDI sandbox with a sample identity. No state-issued ID in this build.
  • Where the proof is madeIn the investor's browser tab, on her own computer. The official wallet does not produce this proof today. The chain does not trust our server.
  • SimulatedSanctions and similar checks are stubs. The second attested wallet is attested by the operator route with a simulated presentation. The card onramp button is disabled.
  • Manual revocationThe issuer withdraws approval by hand. One decision closes subscribe, claim, redeem and the pool.
  • On chainNo identity documents on chain, and nothing we could use to find her. Predicate bits and an expiry bound to an address are still personal data; we say so.
  • Wallet by PrivyAn embedded wallet created when you sign in with email, so you can hold the fund units and the stablecoin without a seed phrase. Privy describes it as self-custodial and never sees your identity evidence; it sees this address and the transactions you confirm. Sample identity from the official test wallet; testnet funds.
