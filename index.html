The turn

An ID you hold yourself.

By the end of 2026 every EU state must offer a digital identity wallet (eIDAS 2, Art. 5a). By accident of law it is an identity document that lives on your phone and answers to you.

  • The state signs. Once. Then it hands the credential to you.
  • You hold. It lives on your phone, not on a server.
  • You choose. Show three fields, keep the rest. Selective disclosure is the default.
  • The provider cannot watch. The law bars the wallet provider from tracking how you use it (eIDAS 2, Art. 5a(16)).

The state becomes a signer, not a watcher. Less state visibility than today, not more.

Deployment

Where the proof lives.

The chain side is final. The registry takes a record in one of two ways: from a zero-knowledge proof checked by the policy's verifier, or signed by the issuer's operator key. The issuer's approval is a separate step in both. Where the proof is made is a deployment choice, and it decides who sees the presentation. In the server route the issuer's verifier sees the presentation, name included, while it makes the proof. In the browser route the issuer's server stores only the wallet's encrypted response and never opens it, so your name stays in your browser tab. In every route the chain gets policy, predicate bits, tier, expiry and a bound address, and never a name.

  1. Route 1, built Our verifier

    The issuer's verifier checks the presentation and generates the zero-knowledge proof (SP1, Groth16). The chain does not trust the server, but the server did see the presentation.

  2. Route 2, built Your device

    Our server is a blind relay that stores only the encrypted wallet response. Your browser tab decrypts it and makes the proof (Noir, UltraHonk). This is the route the demo runs. Same contract, same chain record.

  3. When wallets ship provers The wallet

    The wallet proves on the phone itself. The pre-existing verifier already accepts Longfellow proofs. Nothing on chain changes.

Three routes, one interface.

Why now

2027

The wallet arrives. Finance has to accept it.

Member states must offer the wallet by the end of 2026, and banks and financial services must accept it from the end of 2027 (eIDAS 2, Art. 5a and Art. 5f).

Every gated venue today filters by residence and sanctions through its own document funnel. Each funnel is another copy.

Attestat lets the issuer accept the state wallet's evidence and apply its approval to the investor's linked crypto wallet, without putting a document on chain. The issuer keeps its file; the chain keeps a decision.
